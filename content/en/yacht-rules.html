How to play Yacht Dice

Roll five dice, build combinations, and fill in twelve boxes on your scorecard. Each round works like this:

  1. Roll all five dice.
  2. Keep the dice you like by tapping them. Kept dice stay put when you roll again.
  3. Reroll the rest. You get three rolls per round, including the first one.
  4. Score by picking one empty box. Once a box is filled it is locked for the rest of the game.

The game ends after twelve rounds. You may score a box even when the dice do not match it — you simply take a zero. Deciding which box to sacrifice, and when, is the heart of the game.

Categories and scoring

Upper section (1 to 6)

Each box scores the total of that number only. Three 4s in the Fours box scores 12 points; the other dice are ignored.

Lower section

CategoryRequirementScore
ChoiceAnythingSum of all five dice
Four of a KindFour or more matching diceSum of all five dice
Full HouseThree of one number plus two of anotherSum of all five dice
Small StraightFour consecutive numbers15 points
Large StraightFive consecutive numbers30 points
YachtAll five dice matching50 points

The 35 point upper bonus

If your six upper boxes add up to 63 or more, you get a 35 point bonus. That number is not arbitrary: 63 is exactly what you score with three of each number (three 1s, three 2s, and so on up to three 6s).

So three of a number is the break-even line for each box. Four 6s puts you six points ahead of pace; only two 2s leaves you two points behind. The bonus row on the scorecard shows how far you still have to go.

Tips for a higher score

  • The bonus is worth almost as much as a Yacht. Filling the high numbers (5s and 6s) properly is usually the most reliable way to secure it, and it is far easier to hit than five of a kind.
  • Decide early which box you are willing to waste. Yacht and Large Straight often never show up. Dumping a bad roll into Aces costs you a couple of points; being forced to zero out Yacht on the last round costs you fifty.
  • Save Choice for a rescue. It accepts any roll at face value, which makes it the ideal place to put a round where nothing came together.
  • Chase straights from the middle. Holding 3-4-5 means either a 2 or a 6 completes a Small Straight. Holding 1-2-3 only works with a 4, so you have half the outs.

The rules this site uses

Yacht rules vary by region. This version follows the convention common in Korea, which differs from American Yahtzee in a couple of places:

  • Four of a Kind and Full House score the sum of all five dice, not a flat 25.
  • Five matching dice also count as a Full House.
  • Play solo, or pass one device back and forth for two players.
  • Free to play with no real money, wagering, or prizes. No install and no sign-up.
